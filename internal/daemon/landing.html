Fly.io for your homelab.

Deploy real apps to your own hardware. oakberryio builds your app, boots it in a Firecracker microVM, waits for its health check, and serves it over a Cloudflare Tunnel. No Kubernetes. No cloud bill.

What you get

A tiny PaaS that runs entirely on your box.

Push an oak.toml and oakberryio handles the build, the microVM, the health check, and the ingress. The parts you'd normally rent, running on hardware you already own.

Firecracker microVMs

Every app runs in its own microVM with a real kernel, not a shared-kernel container. Real isolation, sub-second boots.

oak.toml manifests

Declare your image, ports, env, volumes and VM size in one file — the same idea as fly.toml. Deploy with a single command.

Tunnel ingress

Each app gets a public hostname through a Cloudflare Tunnel. Zero open ports, automatic TLS, custom domains when you want them.

Persistent volumes

Attach a volume and run stateful apps — a real Postgres with its data on disk survives redeploys and reboots.

oak ssh

Drop straight into a shell inside any running microVM over vsock — no sshd, no keys to manage, no ports exposed.

Per-tenant isolation

Give friends access and each tenant's apps get their own subnet and bridge — no one reaches another tenant's services or your databases.

How it works

Deploy in three steps.

  1. 1Turn a fresh Ubuntu Server into a box with one command.
  2. 2Install the CLI on your laptop.
  3. 3Run oak deploy from any app with an oak.toml.

That's the whole loop. Build, boot, health-check, cut over — zero downtime, every deploy.

# 1. on a fresh Ubuntu Server box
curl -fsSL oakberryio.iagocavalcante.com/box.sh \
  | sudo OAK_DOMAIN=apps.example.com bash

# 2. install the CLI on your laptop
curl -fsSL oakberryio.iagocavalcante.com/install.sh | sh

# 3. from your app directory
oak deploy
ok hello → hello.apps.example.com

Multi-tenant, safely

Give friends a box, not your database.

Every owner's apps land on their own subnet and Linux bridge. Cross-tenant traffic is routed through the host and dropped, and internal DNS only resolves your own apps. Invite people in without handing over the keys.

  • Per-tenant subnets and bridges
  • Source-scoped internal DNS
  • GitHub-auth'd web control panel

Stateful apps

Real volumes, real Postgres.

Declare a mount in your oak.toml and oakberryio gives the microVM a persistent disk. Run a database on your own metal, back it up, and keep the data across every redeploy.

  • Persistent volumes attached at any path
  • TCP or HTTP health checks before cutover
  • Release commands for migrations

Bring your own hardware

Your homelab is a platform now.

An old mini-PC, a spare server, a Raspberry-class board with KVM — if it runs Linux and Firecracker, it runs oakberryio. Grab the CLI and deploy something tonight.
